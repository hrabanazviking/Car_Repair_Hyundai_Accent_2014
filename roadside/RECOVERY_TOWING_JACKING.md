# Recovery, Towing, and Jacking

**Vehicle focus:** U.S.-market 2014 Hyundai Accent SE, front-wheel drive, 6-speed automatic

**Purpose:** Safe roadside lifting, tire-change jacking, commercial towing, emergency short-distance towing, and low-force self-recovery guidance for a low-clearance front-wheel-drive Accent.

> **Core rule:** A stuck car is usually cheaper than a broken car, and a broken car is always cheaper than an injured person.

---

## Source / confidence policy

This guide separates exact 2014 Hyundai owner-manual instructions from general recovery practice.

- **EXACT-2014:** directly supported by 2014 Hyundai Accent owner documentation or 2014 parts data.
- **CORROBORATED:** supported by same-generation Hyundai material or multiple reputable sources.
- **GENERAL-SAFETY:** conservative automotive/recovery practice not presented as an exact Hyundai specification.
- **UNKNOWN:** not verified well enough to state as an exact fact.

When exact Hyundai instructions and generic recovery folklore disagree, use the Hyundai instruction.

---

## 1. The three jobs that people wrongly mix together

### 1.1 Jacking

Jacking means lifting part of the vehicle vertically so a wheel can be removed or service performed.

The factory jack is intended for **emergency tire changing only**.

### 1.2 Towing

Towing means transporting or pulling a disabled but freely rolling vehicle along a road.

The factory emergency tow eye is intended for **temporary towing**, under tightly limited conditions.

### 1.3 Recovery

Recovery means extracting a vehicle that is **stuck**, such as in:

- mud
- sand
- snow
- a rut
- a ditch edge
- a hole
- a soft shoulder
- a high-centered condition

Recovery can impose much higher and less predictable loads than ordinary towing.

**Do not assume equipment safe for towing is automatically safe for recovery.**

# 2. Exact 2014 Hyundai jacking rules

Hyundai's 2014 owner manual states that the jack is provided for emergency tire changing only.

Source:

- https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1067849/Hyundai-2014-Accent.html
- https://manuals.plus/m/28181eb364b39dbbb16f909d42f8e060859e68749899494eb6c5fca29f35b8b1
- https://www.carmanualsonline.info/hyundai-accent-2014-owner-s-manual

## 2.1 Before lifting

**EXACT-2014:**

1. Park on a **level surface**.
2. Apply the parking brake firmly.
3. For the automatic transmission, place the selector in **P (Park)**.
4. Turn on the hazard flashers.
5. Remove the jack, jack handle, lug wrench, and spare tire.
6. Block **both the front and rear** of the wheel diagonally opposite the wheel being changed.
7. Loosen each lug nut about one turn before lifting.

Hyundai specifically warns against jacking on unstable ground.

If a firm, level location cannot be found, the owner manual advises calling a towing service.

---

## 2.2 Factory jacking locations

**EXACT-2014:** Use only the designated front or rear jacking point closest to the wheel being changed.

Hyundai describes the factory points as designated locations under the frame and warns not to use:

- bumpers
- random floor-pan locations
- plastic rocker trim
- other body parts

Source illustration and procedure:

- https://www.dezosmanuals.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/2014-Hyundai-Accent-OM.pdf
- https://manuals.plus/m/28181eb364b39dbbb16f909d42f8e060859e68749899494eb6c5fca29f35b8b1

The exact jacking-point geometry is shown graphically in the owner manual. When working physically on the car, verify the reinforced pinch-weld/jacking feature visually rather than assuming a nearby thin flange is equivalent.

### Plastic-guard warning

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai warns that incorrect jack placement can damage the plastic guard near the jacking point.

---

## 2.3 Lift only as high as required

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai says to raise the vehicle until the tire just clears the ground, approximately **1.2 in / 30 mm**.

Before removing the wheel, verify that the vehicle is stable and has no tendency to move or slip.

---

# 3. Never work under a car supported only by a jack

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai explicitly warns:

- do not place any part of the body under a vehicle supported only by the jack
- use vehicle support stands
- do not start or run the engine while the vehicle is on the jack
- do not allow anyone to remain inside the vehicle while it is being jacked

This applies regardless of whether the jack is:

- the factory scissor jack
- a bottle jack
- a floor jack
- another hydraulic jack

A hydraulic jack can lower unexpectedly from leakage, seal failure, valve movement, shifting ground, or vehicle movement.

---

# 4. Bottle jack guidance

A 2-ton bottle jack may have adequate nominal load capacity for the Accent, but **capacity alone does not make it safe at a particular lift point**.

## 4.1 Requirements before using a bottle jack

**GENERAL-SAFETY:**

Use a bottle jack only when all of the following are true:

- the jack is rated above the expected corner load
- the saddle contacts a structurally suitable point
- the saddle cannot slip from the contact point
- the ground is firm and level
- the jack remains vertical through the lift
- the jack has enough minimum height to fit before lifting
- the jack has enough stroke without requiring improvised stacking

### Do not place a narrow bottle-jack saddle directly against thin sheet metal or plastic trim.

A pinch-weld adapter or suitable load-spreading saddle may be required, but any adapter itself must be stable and appropriately rated.

## 4.2 Soft ground

The owner manual requires firm, level ground.

If stranded on dirt or gravel and lifting cannot be avoided, a broad, strong, flat jack base may reduce sink-in risk, but this **does not transform soft or sloped terrain into factory-approved jacking ground**.

Never use loose stacks of:

- rocks
- firewood
- scrap metal
- bricks
- unstable boards

as a jack pedestal.

If the surface cannot reliably support the jack, use recovery/towing assistance instead.

# 6. Commercial towing: preferred method

Hyundai recommends professional towing when towing is required.

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai says proper lifting and towing procedures are necessary to avoid vehicle damage and recommends wheel dollies or flatbed equipment.

Source:

- https://manuals.plus/m/28181eb364b39dbbb16f909d42f8e060859e68749899494eb6c5fca29f35b8b1

## 6.1 Front-wheel-drive rule

The 2014 Accent is front-wheel drive.

**EXACT-2014:**

- It is acceptable for commercial towing to have the **rear wheels on the ground and front wheels lifted**.
- If front wheels would otherwise remain on the ground, use a **towing dolly under the front wheels**.
- When dollies are not used, the **front of the vehicle should be lifted, not the rear**.
- Do not tow the vehicle backward with the front wheels on the ground.
- Do not use sling-type towing equipment.

### Best default

For an unknown failure, underbody damage, suspension damage, transmission trouble, or remote-road incident, a **flatbed** is the safest default transport method.

# 7. Emergency four-wheels-down towing

Emergency tow-eye towing is not the preferred normal transport method.

Hyundai limits it heavily.

## 7.1 Preparation

**EXACT-2014:** When towing without wheel dollies:

1. Ignition switch to **ACC**.
2. Transmission in **N (Neutral)**.
3. Parking brake released.

Failure to select Neutral can damage the transmission.

Source:

- https://www.carmanualsonline.info/hyundai-accent-2014-owner-s-manual/?srch=emergency+release

## 7.2 Automatic-transmission limit

**EXACT-2014:** If the automatic-transmission Accent is towed with all four wheels on the ground:

- tow only from the **front**
- transmission must be in **Neutral**
- steering must be unlocked
- a driver must remain in the Accent to steer and brake
- maximum towing speed: **10 mph / 15 km/h**
- towing distance: **less than 1 mile / 1.5 km**

If automatic-transmission fluid is leaking, Hyundai requires flatbed equipment or a towing dolly.

Source:

- https://manuals.plus/m/28181eb364b39dbbb16f909d42f8e060859e68749899494eb6c5fca29f35b8b1
- https://manualzz.com/doc/53857913/hyundai-2014-accent-owner-manual

### Interpretation

This is a **last-resort short-distance emergency maneuver**, not a method for moving the car several miles to a shop or campsite.

**EXACT-2014:**

2. Remove the bumper tow-hole cover.
3. Thread the hook clockwise until fully secured.

# 9. What the factory tow hook IS for

The factory emergency tow hook is intended for **temporary towing of a vehicle that can roll normally**.

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai says emergency tow-hook towing is appropriate only:

- on hard-surfaced roads
- for a short distance
- at low speed
- when wheels, axles, drivetrain, steering, and brakes are in good condition

Source:

- https://manuals.plus/m/28181eb364b39dbbb16f909d42f8e060859e68749899494eb6c5fca29f35b8b1

# 10. What the factory tow hook is NOT for

This is one of the most important safety sections in the repository.

## 10.1 No stuck-vehicle extraction

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai explicitly says:

> Do not use the tow hooks to pull a vehicle out of mud, sand or other conditions from which the vehicle cannot be driven out under its own power.

Source:

- https://manuals.plus/m/28181eb364b39dbbb16f909d42f8e060859e68749899494eb6c5fca29f35b8b1
- https://manualzz.com/doc/53857913/hyundai-2014-accent-owner-manual

## 10.2 No jerking

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai instructs:

- do not jerk the hook
- apply steady and even force
- do not pull from the side
- do not pull at a vertical angle
- pull straight ahead
- if the vehicle cannot be moved, do not forcibly continue

That rules out high-energy snatch-style recovery from the factory tow eye.

---

# 11. Kinetic ropes and the Accent

A kinetic recovery rope works by stretching and storing energy before releasing it into the stuck vehicle.

That loading mode can impose **far greater peak force** than a gentle static tow.

## Hard rule

```text
FACTORY ACCENT TOW EYE
        +
KINETIC / SNATCH RECOVERY
        =
DO NOT USE
```

This conclusion is not guesswork. Hyundai specifically prohibits jerking the hook and prohibits using the hook to extract the vehicle from mud or sand.

A kinetic rope should be used only when both vehicles have **properly engineered, verified, rated recovery points** appropriate for kinetic recovery.

### Do not assume any of these are rated recovery points:

- removable threaded tow eye
- transport tie-down loop
- suspension control arm
- sway bar
- axle/CV shaft
- bumper reinforcement
- radiator support
- engine cradle hole
- ball joint
- random chassis opening

Unless a point has a verified rated recovery purpose, treat it as **unrated**.

---

# 12. Soft shackles

Soft shackles are useful only when the attachment points themselves are appropriate.

A soft shackle does **not** make an unrated tow eye into a rated recovery point.

Do not place a soft shackle over:

- sharp unfinished sheet-metal edges
- cutting edges
- hot exhaust components
- moving suspension parts
- points that can crush or pinch the fibers

Inspect for abrasion, melted fibers, cuts, fuzzing, chemical damage, and contamination before use.

---

# 13. Recovery decision tree

```text
VEHICLE STUCK
    ↓
ANY INJURY / TRAFFIC / FIRE / WATER HAZARD?
    ├─ YES → EMERGENCY SERVICES / PROFESSIONAL RECOVERY
    └─ NO
         ↓
IS VEHICLE STRUCTURALLY DAMAGED OR HIGH-CENTERED?
    ├─ YES → PROFESSIONAL RECOVERY
    └─ NO
         ↓
CAN IT EXIT UNDER ITS OWN POWER WITH LOW-FORCE HELP?
    ├─ YES → DIG / TRACTION BOARDS / GENTLE ROCKING
    └─ NO
         ↓
IS THERE A VERIFIED RATED RECOVERY POINT?
    ├─ NO → DO NOT KINETICALLY RECOVER
    └─ YES → FOLLOW EQUIPMENT/RATING PROCEDURES
```

# 14. Traction boards

For the Accent, traction boards are often a much better first recovery tool than pulling on the car.

They keep the extraction force within the tire/ground interface instead of applying large external loads to body attachment points.

## 14.1 Suitable situations

Traction boards may help when:

- drive wheels are spinning on shallow mud
- drive wheels are in loose sand
- packed snow or ice prevents initial movement
- one front wheel is in a small rut
- the vehicle can still move slightly under its own power

## 14.2 Procedure

**GENERAL-SAFETY:**

1. Stop spinning the tires.
2. Determine the intended escape direction.
3. Dig loose material away from the front of the drive tires.
4. Reduce the climb angle where possible.
5. Place the boards firmly into contact with the tires.
6. Keep people away from the board trajectory.
7. Apply light, progressive throttle.
8. Stop if wheels begin spinning aggressively.
9. Reposition and try again rather than building wheel speed.

### Front-wheel drive

The Accent's front tires are the driven tires, so traction assistance normally belongs under the front tires in the intended direction of travel.

---

# 15. Digging out

A shovel is often safer than more throttle.

Remove material from:

- in front of the driven tires
- behind the tires if reversing is safer
- under the front bumper lip if it is plowing soil/snow
- beneath any underbody component that is high-centered

## High-centered warning

If the vehicle's body, subframe, exhaust, suspension, oil pan area, or another structural/mechanical component is carrying vehicle weight, spinning the wheels may accomplish nothing and can increase damage.

If the car is substantially high-centered, professional recovery may be the safest option.

---

# 16. Rocking the vehicle free

The 2014 owner manual permits attempting to rock a vehicle stuck in snow, mud, or sand by alternating forward and reverse, but warns against prolonged wheel spinning and overheating.

Source:

- https://manualzz.com/doc/53857913/hyundai-2014-accent-owner-manual

## Conservative automatic-transmission method

1. Straighten the wheels when possible.
2. Turn ESC off if Hyundai's procedure calls for it for rocking.
3. Apply light throttle.
4. Allow the vehicle to roll back naturally before shifting direction.
5. Come to a near/complete stop before selecting the opposite gear.
6. Avoid rapid D-to-R or R-to-D shifts under load.
7. Stop if transmission temperature, coolant temperature, tire temperature, smell, or noise becomes concerning.

### Do not spin tires at high speed

Hyundai warns that excessive tire spin can overheat and damage tires and can damage the transmission/engine.

# 17. Mud recovery

Mud creates both traction loss and suction.

For the Accent:

- stop before digging the front tires deeper
- clear mud from ahead of the drive tires
- clear packed mud around suspension and underbody
- use traction boards
- choose the shallowest exit line
- avoid full-throttle wheel spin

Do not attach a kinetic rope to the factory tow eye to defeat mud suction.

Hyundai specifically prohibits using the tow hook for this purpose.

---

# 18. Sand recovery

Sand rewards momentum but punishes wheel spin.

For this low-clearance front-wheel-drive vehicle:

- avoid entering deep loose sand when a firm alternative exists
- preserve momentum gently
- if bogged, stop early
- dig around the front tires
- place traction boards
- reduce required climb angle

Do not blindly lower tire pressure below Hyundai's placard pressure merely because off-road advice says to "air down." The SE uses relatively low-profile 195/50R16 tires, and aggressive pressure reduction can increase rim-impact and sidewall damage risk.

See:

- `../tires-wheels/TIRES_WHEELS.md`

---

# 19. Snow and ice recovery

Priorities:

1. Clear snow from beneath the chassis.
2. Clear a path in front of the driven tires.
3. Apply traction material or boards.
4. Use smooth throttle.
5. Avoid wheel spin.

If the car is resting on packed snow beneath the chassis, traction aids may not work until that snow is removed.

---

# 20. Water and ditch hazards

Do not perform improvised recovery when:

- the vehicle is in moving water
- water may rise
- the vehicle could roll or slide farther
- the vehicle is unstable on a steep bank
- fuel is leaking
- high-voltage utility lines are nearby
- recovery would place people downhill/in-line with a loaded rope

Professional recovery is appropriate.

---

# 21. Recovery line danger zone

Any loaded towing or recovery line can fail.

Keep people away from:

- directly in front of either vehicle
- directly behind either vehicle
- the line between vehicles
- the likely travel path of hooks, shackles, or hardware

A heavy metal hook or shackle attached to a failed recovery system can become a projectile.

The safest recovery system minimizes both stored energy and loose heavy hardware.

---

# 22. Never attach recovery equipment to suspension or drivetrain parts

Do not improvise attachment to:

- tie rods
- control arms
- ball joints
- struts
- springs
- torsion beam
- CV axles
- sway bar
- steering rack
- exhaust system
- engine/transmission mounts

These are not substitute recovery points.

Damage may not be immediately visible and can later cause loss of steering or wheel control.

---

# 23. Flatbed loading after underbody damage

After striking a rock, rut, stump, curb, or road crown, inspect before allowing the vehicle to be pulled onto a flatbed.

Look for:

- oil leak
- coolant leak
- fuel leak
- transmission fluid leak
- bent wheel
- tire sidewall damage
- hanging exhaust
- displaced suspension
- damaged brake hose
- loose splash shield

If a wheel is locked or suspension is collapsed, inform the tow operator before winching.

Skates/dollies may be necessary to avoid dragging a locked wheel.

---

# 24. Towing after transmission-fluid leakage

**EXACT-2014:** If automatic-transmission fluid is leaking, Hyundai says to use a flatbed or towing dolly.

Do not attempt four-wheels-down emergency towing with known ATF loss.

See:

- `../transmission/SIX_SPEED_AUTOMATIC.md`

---

# 25. Towing after wheel or suspension damage

Hyundai's towing instructions say that if loaded wheels or suspension components are damaged, use appropriate towing equipment rather than letting the damaged wheel carry road load.

### Examples requiring extra caution

- broken ball joint
- severely bent control arm
- failed wheel bearing
- broken tie rod
- collapsed strut
- wheel pushed rearward in arch
- wheel visibly tilted
- seized brake/wheel

Do not assume a damaged wheel can safely roll onto a tow truck under its own load.

---

# 26. Braking and steering during emergency tow-eye towing

**EXACT-2014:** With the engine off during emergency towing:

- braking effort will be higher than normal
- steering effort will be higher because power steering assistance is unavailable

A driver must remain in the towed car to steer and brake.

Long downhill towing can overheat the brakes.

This is another reason flatbed transport is preferable.

---

# 27. Tow strap length for Hyundai emergency towing

**EXACT-2014:** Hyundai specifies a towing strap/cable less than **16 ft / 5 m** long and calls for a visible cloth marker about **12 in / 30 cm** wide at the center.

This belongs to Hyundai's low-speed emergency road-towing procedure, not stuck-vehicle snatch recovery.

Source:

- https://manualzz.com/doc/53857913/hyundai-2014-accent-owner-manual

# 28. Static tow vs kinetic recovery

## Static/steady tow

Force rises relatively gradually.

Hyundai's factory tow-eye instructions are written around this concept:

- steady force
- straight pull
- no jerking
- freely rolling vehicle
- hard surface

## Kinetic recovery

Force is deliberately stored in a stretching rope and released rapidly.

This is **not compatible with the factory tow-eye instructions**.

```text
STATIC TOWING OF FREE-ROLLING CAR
        MAY USE FACTORY TOW EYE
        WITHIN HYUNDAI LIMITS

STUCK-VEHICLE EXTRACTION
        DO NOT USE FACTORY TOW EYE

KINETIC SNATCH
        DO NOT USE FACTORY TOW EYE
```

---

# 29. Winching

A winch can provide more controlled force than a kinetic pull, but the attachment point still matters.

The factory tow-eye warning against pulling a car from mud/sand remains applicable even if the force comes from a winch rather than another vehicle.

Do not assume that "slow pull" converts the tow eye into a recovery point.

A professional operator may use specialized methods such as wheel-lift, bridle, skates, or approved structural attachment techniques.

---

# 30. Low-clearance vehicle strategy

The Accent is not an off-road vehicle.

A successful recovery plan begins before the car gets stuck.

Turn around when the road requires:

- more ground clearance than the car has
- deep rut crossing
- large embedded rock clearance
- deep mud
- deep loose sand
- severe washouts
- large water crossings

The best recovery tool is often an early decision not to enter.

# 32. Jack stands in field repairs

If a repair requires placing any part of the body beneath the vehicle, jack stands or another positively rated support method are required.

### Do not crawl under the car for:

- exhaust repair
- splash-shield repair
- oil-pan inspection
- brake-line work
- suspension inspection
- transmission inspection

while the car is supported only by a jack.

If safe stand placement cannot be established on firm level ground, postpone the repair and recover the vehicle.

---

# 33. Underbody inspection after recovery

After leaving mud, sand, ruts, or a ditch, inspect:

## Engine / transmission

- oil pan
- oil leaks
- transmission case
- ATF leaks
- lower engine covers

## Steering / suspension

- tie rods
- ball joints
- control arms
- struts
- CV boots
- wheel alignment appearance

## Brakes

- brake hoses
- ABS wiring
- caliper damage

## Exhaust

- crushed pipe
- loose hangers
- contact with body

## Cooling

- radiator/condenser area
- lower hoses
- coolant leakage

## Wheels / tires

- bent rim
- sidewall cuts
- embedded rocks
- bead damage

# 34. Stop-driving conditions after a recovery event

Do not continue driving normally if any of these are present:

- engine oil leak
- transmission fluid leak
- fuel leak
- coolant leak
- brake-fluid leak
- steering geometry visibly changed
- tire sidewall cut/bulge
- wheel visibly bent
- grinding wheel bearing
- brake hose stretched/damaged
- CV axle displaced
- severe vibration
- underbody structure visibly deformed
- exhaust touching fuel/brake lines or body in a hazardous way

Recover the vehicle rather than converting one incident into a second failure.

# 35. AI diagnostic / recovery rules

An AI using this repository should follow these rules.

## Rule 1: Identify the job

Before recommending equipment, classify the situation as:

- jack/lift
- roadside tire change
- normal tow
- emergency short tow
- stuck-vehicle recovery

## Rule 2: Do not confuse tow points with recovery points

The factory Accent tow eye is an **emergency towing point**, not a verified kinetic-recovery point.

## Rule 3: Prefer low-energy recovery

For a lightly stuck Accent, prioritize:

1. stop wheel spin
2. dig
3. clear underbody
4. traction boards
5. gentle rocking
6. professional recovery if unsuccessful

## Rule 4: Never recommend kinetic load on the factory tow eye

Hyundai says:

- do not jerk the hook
- do not use it to pull the car from mud/sand
- use steady force
- pull straight

Therefore kinetic/snatch loading is incompatible with the documented use case.

## Rule 5: Prefer flatbed for uncertain mechanical damage

Especially when there is:

- ATF leakage
- suspension damage
- wheel damage
- steering damage
- locked wheel
- collision/underbody damage

## Rule 6: No body beneath a jack-only vehicle

This is a hard safety rule.

## Rule 7: Do not invent attachment ratings

If the working-load limit or recovery rating of a point is unknown, mark it **UNKNOWN / UNRATED**.

Unknown is safer than confident fiction.
